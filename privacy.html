Privacy policy

Last updated 16 November 2026. This policy covers the BoxTime app for Android and its Wear OS companion (package uk.jj.boxtime) and this website. BoxTime is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Your timers, your history and your settings are kept on your phone. They are never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • BoxTime has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • A session needs no internet. The app goes online only to check for a newer version, and the first time you open the QR scanner. The detail is below.

What stays on your phone

BoxTime keeps your settings, your saved timers and your session history in the app's private storage. History holds, for each session, when it started, the name of the timer, how many rounds you finished and how long you spent in rounds. Nothing else about you is recorded: no name, no weight, no heart rate and no location.

If you add a sound of your own, a copy of that file is kept in the app's private storage. A timer leaves your phone only when you choose to share it, as a QR code on your screen or as a file sent with Share, and what is shared is that one timer: its name, its times and its round names.

BoxTime does not ask for your camera, your microphone, your location, your contacts or access to your files. When you pick a sound, a backup or a shared timer, Android's own file picker hands BoxTime that one file. Android's list of what the app may do also shows running at start-up and viewing network connections. Both come with the standard building block that draws the home-screen widget; BoxTime starts no session at start-up.

GitHub also receives your device's internet address, as any online service does. The scanner and, on most phones, the voice are provided by Google under the Google Privacy Policy; how the voice handles text depends on the speech engine your phone uses. The developer of BoxTime receives none of this.

The watch app

The Wear OS companion talks only to BoxTime on the phone it is paired with, over the link between your watch and your phone that Google Play services provides. The phone tells the watch where the clock is, and the watch tells the phone when you press back, pause or skip. The watch app stores nothing and has no internet access of its own.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Sessions stay in History until you remove them one at a time or choose Clear all history.
  • Saved timers stay until you delete them in Timers. Sounds you added stay until you remove them from the sound list.
  • A backup file you save is an ordinary file, kept wherever you chose to save it. It holds your timers, history and settings, and is yours to keep or delete.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling BoxTime deletes everything it holds.
  • If your phone's own backup is switched on, Android may include BoxTime's data in it. That backup is yours and is managed in your phone's settings.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of BoxTime holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. Your timers and history are on your own phone, where you control them directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

The app's data is held in private storage that other apps cannot read. The app's connection to GitHub uses HTTPS.

A timer that arrives by QR code or file is checked before it is used. BoxTime reads only the times and names in it, keeps them within sensible limits, and asks you before adding it.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other websites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app files are downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

BoxTime is a general-purpose training timer. It is not directed at children and its developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
